# Game-first learning apps for ages 4-7, and the Danish/Nordic market

Research pass 2026-10-13, 16 tool calls. Several store pages and articles could not be fetched (404/403), so some
detail comes from search-result summaries rather than full pages; this is flagged where it matters. Items marked
"(prior knowledge, not re-verified)" are offered as leads only and carry no citation.

## 1. Core loops: how the learning act IS the game action, and what makes it replayable

### Takeaway
The strongest designs turn the learning object itself into something the child handles: numbers become creatures or
blocks you stack, slice and combine (DragonBox Numbers, Fiete Math, Numberblocks), and letters become things you eat or
catch in a world (Poio). Replayability comes from a world that grows (Moose Math's town, Poio's houses and storybook,
Fiete's harbours and ships) or from a toy with no end state (Pok Pok, Toca Boca), not from scores.

### Cited Findings

**DragonBox Numbers / Kahoot! Numbers by DragonBox (Oslo, WeWantToKnow → Kahoot!)**
- Numbers are turned into colourful characters called **Nooms** that can be "stacked, sliced, combined, sorted,
  compared, and played with"; the stated aim is intuitive number sense rather than repetition and memorisation; ages
  4-8 — [Games for Change](https://www.gamesforchange.org/games/dragonbox-numbers/)
- Four activities: **Sandbox** (free experimentation with Nooms), **Puzzle** (basic maths reveals a hidden picture),
  **Ladder** (build bigger numbers from smaller ones), **Run** (quick mental calculation); addition and subtraction 1-20
  — [Games for Change](https://www.gamesforchange.org/games/dragonbox-numbers/); [Skich listing](https://skich.app/games/kahoot-numbers-by-dragonbox)
- Released c. 2016; now sold as "Kahoot! Numbers by DragonBox" inside the Kahoot! Kids subscription; DragonBox BIG Numbers
  reuses the Nooms for long addition/subtraction — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1529174508); [App Store (old listing)](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/dragonbox-numbers/id1005050125)
- Founded by maths teacher Jean-Baptiste Huynh and cognitive scientist Patrick Marchal — [DragonBox: Our story](https://dragonbox.com/about/our-story)

**Kahoot! Learn to Read by Poio (Norway)**
- Story: the troll Poio has stolen a storybook from the **Readlings**, bugs that eat letters. The child guides
  Readlings through Straw Island, forest, desert and winter worlds; each level's goal is to eat vowels and find a new
  word for the book. Trapped Readlings are freed by solving letter-sound and spelling tasks. Each rescue unlocks a
  **house** the child decorates with coins earned, practising nouns/verbs. Collectible cards reward exploration.
  **Mastered words are written into a fairy-tale story the child can read aloud.** Adapts to skill — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1529174573)
- Design philosophy: "few instructions, no negative feedback, and exploration as the core interface", a "true" gaming
  experience rather than digitised school books; letters and sounds introduced gradually as the child explores — [Kahoot! blog, 2019](https://kahoot.com/blog/2019/05/15/poio-joins-kahoot-family-innovative-learn-to-read-app/); [DE Magazine](https://www.demagazine.co.uk/?p=2866)
- Languages listed: English, French, German, Italian, Norwegian Bokmål, Portuguese, Spanish, Swedish — **no Danish**.
  Requires a Kahoot! Kids subscription ($5.99/mo, $35.99/yr in US store) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1529174573)

**Fiete Math (Ahoiii Entertainment, Germany)**
- Montessori-based: numbers become touchable objects children group, combine and cut up; tasks are harbour scenes where
  you **load ships with boxes** that group and separate like building blocks; 1,000+ sequential tasks up to 30; new
  countries/harbours and bigger ships unlock; **no time limits** — [Fatherly](https://www.fatherly.com/gear/fiete-apps-by-ahoiii-are-educational-open-ended-games); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1052109508)
- Sister title Fiete Choice won a Red Dot "Best of the Best" for game design — [Fatherly](https://www.fatherly.com/gear/fiete-apps-by-ahoiii-are-educational-open-ended-games)

**Moose Math (Duck Duck Moose)**
- Five mini-games inside a little town the child helps build: smoothie shop (counting, addition, subtraction), pet shop
  (bingo, dot matching), Lost & Found (shapes, colours, geometry), dot-to-dot (skip counting by 1s/2s/5s/10s); finishing
  levels unlocks town upgrades — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/moose-math-by-duck-duck-moose); [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/moose-math-by-duck-duck-moose)
- Critique: kids "may wish there were more parts of the town to build"; a teacher reviewer called it good review/practice
  but not adequate as an introduction to the skills — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/moose-math-by-duck-duck-moose)

**Numberblocks World (Blue Zoo / Alphablocks Ltd, UK)**
- Counting mode progresses from 1s to 2s, 5s, 10s; three **subitising** games; a quiz hosted by Numberblock Six checks
  readiness to move on; the 90-episode series in 5 levels; made with NCETM; core age 4-6; free + subscription — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1520827387)

**Pok Pok Playroom (Canada) and Toca Boca (Sweden) — open-ended toys, no failure**
- Pok Pok: co-founder Esther Huybreghts compares it to a toy car or doll — no "right way to play" and no "finished"
  state; CEO Melissa Cash says win-lose mechanics can harm self-esteem; principle: a child must navigate without adult
  help, and "if a child gets stuck, we've made a mistake"; born from frustration with overstimulating gamified apps — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/?p=332907); [Cubby at Home](https://www.cubbyathome.com/pok-pok-playroom-kids-app-review-80027995); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/pok-pok-playroom-mobile-app-lets-kids-engage-in-open-ended-play/)
- Toca Boca: open-ended, gender-neutral digital toys; historically no ads or IAP; 30+ apps, 100M+ downloads in 215
  countries (2016 figure) — [Motionographer, 2016](https://motionographer.com/2016/04/27/the-design-process-behind-toca-bocas-infectious-apps/). "No winning, no losing, no stress, no high scores, no rules" is a company self-description from a weak secondary page — [readwriterespond](https://readwriterespond.com/2018/02/toca-boca/)

**GraphoGame (Finland, University of Jyväskylä / Grapho Group)**
- Teaches letters, syllables and words by phonics with level design that adapts to the child; 14 languages; Norwegian
  version built with the Lesesenteret, University of Stavanger — [Education Finland](https://educationfinland.fi/member/graphogame); [App Store: GraphoGame Lær norsk](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1568501173); [UNRIC (Danish)](https://unric.org/da/graphogame-finsk-app-mod-analfabetisme/)

### Inferences
- The recurring pattern for "game-first": the **manipulable object is the curriculum unit** (a Noom is 5; a box on Fiete's
  ship is 1 of 10; a Readling eats the letter). Answering is a physical act on that object (drag, stack, slice, feed),
  not picking from a list. The app's current "tap the right answer" pattern is the opposite end of that spectrum.
- Two replay engines recur: (a) **a world the child's play builds/decorates** (Moose Math town, Poio houses, Fiete
  ships) and (b) **a toy with no end state** (DragonBox Sandbox, Pok Pok). Poio's "your mastered words become a
  storybook you can read" is a collection that *is* learning, unlike a sticker that is unrelated to the content.
- (Prior knowledge, not re-verified) DragonBox Algebra 5+ hides algebra as a box-isolation puzzle with pictures that are
  gradually replaced by symbols; Todo Math (Enuma) has short daily missions with drag-and-trace input and strong
  accessibility; Lumi/Marbel is an Indonesian app family of small tap games — none were sourced in this pass.

### Gaps
- No primary design talk (GDC/Nordic Game) for DragonBox Numbers or Poio was retrieved; the Quartz DragonBox article
  returned 403.
- No sources found for Todo Math, Prodigy, Matific, Bedtime Math, Lumi/Marbel, Sago Mini in this pass.
- DR Ramasjang LÆR App Store page returned 404 — current game list unverified (see §3).

## 2. Evidence of learning outcomes and engagement

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly vendor-linked. GraphoGame has the largest research base but a meta-analysis shows very
context-dependent effects; DragonBox has one large Norwegian study reported by its owner with no effect sizes; Poio has
only marketing claims.

### Cited Findings
- **GraphoGame:** a meta-analysis of 19 studies (McTigue et al., 2019, cited secondhand) found word-reading effect sizes
  from **−1.07 to +1.58**, effective only in certain contexts; suspected moderators are orthographic consistency,
  intervention duration and adult support — [Ahmed et al. 2020, Frontiers in Education](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00132/text)
- GraphoGame Rime showed a higher rate of improvement per hour than GG Phoneme in a UK comparison of 31 Grade 2 children;
  a UK RCT secondary analysis looked at 6-7-year-olds above mean play progress — [Ahmed et al. 2020](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/71660)
- A 2024 Indian RCT (54 Grade 2 children, classroom phonics + GraphoLearn) reported gains in letter-sounds, rime units
  and word recognition (summary from the vendor's site) — [GraphoGame group](https://group.graphogame.com/?p=185)
- **DragonBox:** NIFU (Norway) study of ~1,500 children reported better learning outcomes and more positive attitudes;
  the Kahoot! post gives **no effect sizes** and doesn't clearly name the product (2021) — [Kahoot! news](https://kahoot.com/kahoot-news/research-in-norway-shows-dragonbox-delivered-greater-learning-benefits-and-motivation/)
- An AERA 2022 paper on **DragonBox School** (~1,400 grade 1-2 students, quasi-experimental) reported significant
  early-numeracy and motivation gains in grade 1 — [AERA repository](https://www.aera.net/Publications/Online-Paper-Repository/AERA-Online-Paper-Repository-Viewer/ID/1888824)
- Critique: CMU's Aleven and Long argued DragonBox over-scaffolds (prompts steps automatically, never has players write
  their own equations), which may limit transfer — [CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/homepage/computing/2014/spring/fun-and-games.shtml); DragonBox 12+ results are mixed (a Taiwan study found no significant difference) — [NTNU thesis](https://etds.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/thesis/detail/eb81e7a018ae9efb0c5453a719c915ed); [MDSOAR](https://mdsoar.org/items/82bc868c-9f71-4203-995b-377e6efbd798/full)
- **Poio:** "taught over 100,000 children to read" and "award-winning" are vendor claims; no independent study found — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1529174573); [Kahoot! Poio collection](https://kahoot.com/study/collections/learn-to-read-with-poio)

### Inferences
- GraphoGame's moderators matter for this app: Danish orthography is notably inconsistent, which is the context
  in which GraphoGame-style drill showed weaker effects. That is an argument for sound-first, short-word play rather
  than claiming reading outcomes.
- Nothing found supports any specific engagement mechanic (pets, towns, collections) as causing learning; the evidence
  is for practice volume plus phonics/number-sense content. Mechanics should be chosen for fun and session length.

### Gaps
- McTigue et al. (2019) original not fetched; pooled estimate unknown.
- No independent Poio or DragonBox Numbers outcome study located.

## 3. The Danish / Nordic market: what Danish 5-7-year-olds use and what is praised

### Takeaway
There is no Danish Poio and no Danish GraphoGame found. Danish 5-7s mostly meet learning games through DR Ramasjang LÆR
(free, broadcaster), school platforms (Alinea's Matematikfessor), and a long tail of small, older Danish letter-sound
apps (Knæk Læsekoden, Leo og Mona Læsesjov, Hit med lyden, Lolas Alfabettog). A game-first Danish letter or number game
for this age looks like an open niche.

### Cited Findings
- DR replaced its Ramasjang app with three free apps, **Ramasjang KREA, LÆR and LEG**; an adult must log in (DR login) — [mobilsiden.dk](https://mobilsiden.dk/nyheder/apps-sociale-medier/dr-klar-med-tre-nye-ramasjang-apps-til-android-og-ios)
- Ramasjang LÆR (ages 4-8, free) collects games to "practise both numbers and language", including **"Find bogstaver"
  with Hr. Skæg**, animal recognition and memory (from search-result summary; the store page returned 404 on fetch) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6444935660)
- Ramasjang games are built by the Danish studio Dwarf — [dwarf.dk](https://dwarf.dk/work/ramasjang)
- **Matematikfessor (Alinea/Egmont)** is a school platform, grades 0-10: tasks, lessons, activities, printables; vendor
  description — [Alinea](https://www.alinea.dk/matematikfessor-0)
- **Knæk Læsekoden**: most-cited Danish letter-sound app; uses letter *sounds* only, not letter names; marketed as test
  winner, 5/6 hearts in Politiken; reviewer praises the basics but criticises price and limited content — [Børne App Guiden](https://www.boerneappguiden.dk/knaek-laesekoden/); [App Store DK](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/kn%C3%A6k-l%C3%A6sekoden/id455929768?l=da)
- Teacher-facing lists name **Hit med lyden** (Viborg Kommune, free, letter names and sounds), **Stavepladen** (0.-1.
  klasse, 120+ words), **Læserejsen** and **Bogstavernes lyde** (hear a sound, find the letter), **Leo og Mona Læsesjov**
  (free, recommended as more playful than Knæk Læsekoden) — [Dansk Indskoling (CFU)](https://cfuindskolingdansk.wordpress.com/apps/); [Den lille danskhjælper](http://vbsdansk0-2.blogspot.com/p/apps-til-lsetrning.html); [Min Kusine Maria, 2013](https://www.minkusinemaria.dk/2013/12/abc-apps-til-brn-lr-bogstaver-alfabet-lyde-fonem-grafem.html)
- **Lolas Alfabettog** (free, 3-7, letters) is available in Danish — [App Store DK](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/lolas-alfabettog-l%C3%A6r-at-l%C3%A6se/id428422381?l=da)
- Poio's language list excludes Danish — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1529174573); GraphoGame has Norwegian but no Danish version surfaced — [Education Finland](https://educationfinland.fi/member/graphogame)

### Inferences
- The Danish letter apps found are mostly **hear-a-sound → tap-the-letter drills** (Læserejsen, Bogstavernes lyde) —
  the same shape the app already has. The differentiator available is a Poio/DragonBox-style game where the letter or
  number is a character the child acts on, narrated in Danish.
- Knæk Læsekoden's praised trait (sounds, not names) is a concrete Danish-pedagogy signal for a "first sounds" game.
- Many Danish sources are 2013-2020 blogs/teacher pages; treat as older info.

### Gaps
- No Medierådet for Børn og Unge, Børns Vilkår or EMU recommendation list for learning apps was found.
- No usage data on what share of Danish 5-7s use which app.
- Gyldendal, Clio, Rasmus Klump/Bamse apps: nothing retrieved.

## 4. Fit for this app: short endless sessions, non-reader, tap + drag, iPad web PWA, Danish narration

### Takeaway
The designs that fit best are object-manipulation mini-loops with no text and no fail state: Noom-style number
creatures you combine/split (maths), Fiete-style "load the boat to N" (counting/plus), Poio-style "feed the creature the
letter/sound it asks for" with collected words becoming a readable book, and Pok Pok-style toys for colours. Long
narrative/level structures (Poio worlds, Fiete's 1,000 tasks) clash with the no-rounds, no-adaptivity rules.

### Cited Findings
- No-instruction, no-negative-feedback, exploration-first design (Poio) and "if a child gets stuck we made a mistake"
  (Pok Pok) both target pre-readers without adult help — [Kahoot! blog](https://kahoot.com/blog/2019/05/15/poio-joins-kahoot-family-innovative-learn-to-read-app/); [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/?p=332907)
- Fiete Math has no time limits and uses grouping/separating boxes — a drag mechanic that also maps to tap (tap to add a
  box) — [Fatherly](https://www.fatherly.com/gear/fiete-apps-by-ahoiii-are-educational-open-ended-games)
- DragonBox Numbers includes a free Sandbox alongside goal modes — [Games for Change](https://www.gamesforchange.org/games/dragonbox-numbers/)
- Numberblocks World uses subitising games (recognise a quantity at a glance) — short, text-free rounds — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1520827387)

### Inferences
- Candidate core loops for remake, mapped to existing games:
  1. **Number creatures (DragonBox/Numberblocks):** each number 1-10 is a character made of unit blocks; plus = drag two
     together (they merge and the new creature says its Danish name); minus = slice off a piece; compare = the bigger
     creature eats/tips the seesaw. Tap fallback: tap a creature, then tap the target.
  2. **Load the boat (Fiete):** "Båden skal have 7" — drag or tap boxes onto a ten-frame deck; boat sails off when right,
     the next arrives (endless, no round). Covers counting, plus, minus.
  3. **Feed the letter-eater (Poio Readlings):** creature asks with a sound ("sss"), child feeds the matching letter; or
     inverse, child taps a letter and hears its sound. Each "found" word goes into a picture book that the app reads aloud
     — a collection tied to content rather than a separate sticker.
  4. **Paint toy (Pok Pok/Toca):** colour mixing as a sandbox with no wrong answer, with an optional "make this colour"
     request — fits Ram Farven and Nuancer.
- All four are DOM/SVG + drag/tap friendly in a Safari 17 PWA (no native SDK needed); physics-heavy toys (Pok Pok
  marble runs) are the riskiest for performance on the floor device.
- Poio's and GraphoGame's adaptivity and Fiete's long task ladder conflict with the app's no-adaptive-difficulty rule;
  borrow their objects and feedback, not their progression systems.

### Gaps
- No study comparing drag vs tap input for 4-7-year-olds was retrieved in this pass.
- No web/PWA example of these game-first designs found; all cited apps are native.
